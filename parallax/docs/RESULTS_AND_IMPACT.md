## 3. Diagnostics-only baselines (test)

- **A** detects an error in 100% of cases; lexical errors are detected in all 17 invalid-character cases.
- **B** (panic mode) reports 3.85 diagnostics per case on average (max 19) for 1–2 injected errors. 2.66 per case are spurious, and 61.1% of cases have at least one spurious diagnostic. In 94.4% of two-error cases it reports at least two diagnostics.

## 4. Where it works and where it fails (test, P)

| Group | n | C top-1 | P top-1 | P top-3 | P coverage | P wrong & confident |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| structural | 375 | 68.3% | **89.1%** | 92.5% | 66.7% | 3.5% |
| operator-choice | 61 | 34.4% | 44.3% | 47.5% | 6.6% | 3.3% |
| content-lost | 104 | 20.2% | 24.0% | 26.9% | 19.2% | **17.3%** |
| single error | 432 | 61.1% | 76.2% | 78.7% | 55.8% | 4.4% |
| two errors | 108 | 31.5% | 52.8% | 59.3% | 30.6% | 13.0% |

By category: missing `;` 100% (C 94%); missing `{` 100% (C 38%); missing `}` 100% (C 43%); missing comma 100% (C 38%; arity evidence); wrong delimiter 97%; duplicate delimiter 92%. Weak: missing operator 32% (IntelliFix abstains on all 31 instead of guessing); transposition 39% with 25% wrong-confident; missing keyword 60% with 27% wrong-confident; keyword typo 48% with 19% wrong-confident.

**Typical false confident recommendations** (from `failure_examples`). After `while` is deleted, `(b != 0) { … }` parses as an expression statement followed by a block once a `;` is inserted. That is a cheap repair with no competitor, so it is recommended; the margin cannot see that a keyword is missing. Transpositions and keyword typos are similar: a cheap repair yields a different but valid program. These are the main limitation of a syntax-only evidence model.

## 6. Interpretation

- **Improved.** Ranking quality (H1), layout evidence (H2) and confident-error reduction through abstention (H3).
- **Did not improve.** Follow-on stability evidence (H5) gives no measurable top-1 gain; on this benchmark most of its information is already captured by `resolves_all_errors`. AST grouping (H4) yields only a small coverage gain (+1.8 points) and a slightly better AURC. Equivalent-but-different edits (such as parenthesis placement) seem to be uncommon in this benchmark; we did not measure how often they occur.
- **Did not transfer.** τ = 1.5 kept dev selective risk at 5.5%, but test selective risk is 12.0%, above the 10% target. The dev estimate rests on only 164 recommendations from 12 programs, so it is noisy. Dev was not easier overall (dev top-1 65.8% vs test 71.5%), but the cases P recommends on dev happened to be more reliable. A larger dev set, or cross-validation over families, would give a more reliable threshold.
- **Abstention trade-off.** About half of the test cases receive a recommendation. Among the rest, the correct repair is still ranked first in many cases (top-1 is the same with and without abstention); the UI shows it as an alternative rather than a recommendation.
- **CPCT+** recovers the reference less often under our replay. That is expected: it picks arbitrarily among minimum-cost repairs and does not use layout or name evidence. Its replicates agree on the top-1 AST in only 68.6–70.1% of cases (three runs), which illustrates why a confidence signal is needed. This is not evidence that IntelliFix is a better recovery algorithm in CPCT+'s own sense (fewer cascading errors on real code), which we did not measure.

## 7. What additional evidence is needed

1. Real, consented student submissions (different error distribution; multi-token mistakes).
2. Programs from many authors, to stress layout evidence under inconsistent indentation.
3. Learned or cross-validated weights with a proper calibration set, if probability-like confidence is wanted.
4. A user study: do explanations and abstentions help programmers fix code faster?
5. Comparisons on CPCT+'s own metric (number of reported error locations) on real code.
